# "How to Sell IT Services to Enterprise Buyers"
## 3-Hour Online Workshop — Full Training Program

**Trainer:** Borys W. — IT Director | 20 Years in IT | Managing 100-person Engineering Organization
**Format:** Online workshop via Zoom/Teams (10–20 participants)
**Duration:** 3 hours (180 minutes)
**Target Audience:** Sales teams, account managers, delivery managers, and founders of Polish IT outsourcing companies and software houses

---

## Training Philosophy

This training is **not** a typical sales methodology course. It's a masterclass from the **buyer's perspective** — something outsourcing companies almost never get access to. Participants will learn what actually happens inside an enterprise IT organization when they evaluate, select, and manage outsourcing partners. They'll walk away with concrete, actionable changes to their sales process.

## Detailed Session Breakdown

---

### BLOCK 1: THE BUYER'S WORLD (50 minutes)

---

### Session 1.1 — Opening & Context Setting (15 min)

**Type:** Presentation + Interactive Discussion

**Content:**
- Trainer introduction: Who am I, what do I buy, how much do I spend annually on outsourcing
- Quick poll: "How many of you have ever sat in an enterprise IT buyer's chair?" (typically 0–5%)
- The knowledge gap: Why sellers and buyers operate in completely different realities
- Training objectives and what participants will walk away with

**Key talking points:**
- "I receive 5–10 cold outreach messages per week from outsourcing companies. I respond to less than 5%."
- "The average enterprise IT buyer evaluates 6–12 vendors before shortlisting 3. You have ONE chance to make the shortlist."
- "I'm going to show you what happens on my side when your email arrives."

---

### Session 1.2 — Inside the Enterprise Buying Process (20 min)

**Type:** Presentation with Q&A

**Content:**
- How enterprise IT organizations are structured (CTO, VP Engineering, Directors, Managers)
- Who makes the buying decision (hint: it's rarely just one person)
- The typical buying journey: Need identification → Budget approval → Vendor search → Shortlisting → Technical evaluation → Commercial negotiation → Legal/Compliance → Contract signing
- Timelines: Why enterprise deals take 2–6 months (and what you can do to accelerate)
- The role of procurement, legal, and security teams — the "hidden buyers"

**Key talking points:**
- "Your champion inside the company (the person who likes you) needs ammunition to sell you internally. Are you giving them that ammunition?"
- "Procurement's job is to get the best deal. Legal's job is to minimize risk. Security's job is to protect data. You need to satisfy ALL of them."

**Real-world example:** Walk through a real (anonymized) vendor selection process from start to finish.

---

### Session 1.3 — The Buyer's Evaluation Framework (15 min)

**Type:** Presentation + Group Discussion

**Content:**
- Reveal the weighted scoring model buyers use:
  - Technical Fit (20%), Cultural Fit (20%), Track Record (15%), Price/Value (15%), Flexibility (10%), Compliance (10%), Innovation (10%)
- Why price is only 15% — and why most outsourcing companies act like it's 80%
- What "cultural fit" actually means (it's not about ping-pong tables)
- The "veto factors" — things that immediately disqualify you regardless of score

**Interactive element:** Ask participants: "What percentage of your sales pitch focuses on price?" — Compare with the 15% weight buyers give it.

### BLOCK 2: THE 7 DEADLY SINS OF OUTSOURCING SALES (50 minutes)

---

### Session 2.1 — The Live Audit (25 min)

**Type:** Workshop / Group Exercise

**Setup:** Participants break into groups of 3–4 in breakout rooms.

**Exercise: "The Pitch Autopsy"**
1. Each group selects one of the real sales pitches participants brought as pre-work
2. Groups have 10 minutes to evaluate the pitch using the **Buyer's Evaluation Framework** from Session 1.3
3. They must identify: 3 strengths and 3 weaknesses from the buyer's perspective
4. Each group presents their findings (2 min per group)

**Debrief:** Trainer provides live commentary on common patterns observed across groups. Typical findings:
- Too much "about us," not enough "about the client"
- No clear differentiation
- No evidence of outcomes delivered
- Rate card without value justification
- Missing compliance/security information

---

### Session 2.2 — The 7 Deadly Sins Deep Dive (25 min)

**Type:** Presentation with Real Examples

**The 7 Sins:**

**Sin 1: The Generic Outreach**
- Show real anonymized examples of terrible cold emails
- Show the ONE email in 100 that made me respond — and explain why
- Formula: Research + Relevance + Respect for my time

**Sin 2: Selling CVs Instead of Solutions**
- Why "5/5 Java, 4/5 React" means nothing to me
- The power of outcome-based profiles: "Reduced CI/CD pipeline time by 60%"

**Sin 3: The Bait-and-Switch**
- Real stories of companies showing A-players in presales and delivering B-players
- Why this destroys trust permanently and generates negative word-of-mouth

**Sin 4: "We Can Do Everything"**
- Why generalists lose to specialists in enterprise sales
- The paradox: saying "we don't do X" actually increases your credibility

**Sin 5: Hiding Behind Account Managers**
- Why I want to meet the people who will actually do the work
- The tech lead is your best salesperson

**Sin 6: Ignoring Post-Sale Relationship**
- The "sign and disappear" pattern
- How proactive partnership behaviors double contract length

**Sin 7: Competing Only on Price**
- The race to the bottom — and why the cheapest vendor rarely wins the best deals
- How to articulate value that justifies premium rates

### BLOCK 3: HOW TO ACTUALLY WIN — THE PLAYBOOK (50 minutes)

---

### Session 3.1 — Crafting Your Value Proposition (15 min)

**Type:** Presentation + Individual Exercise

**Content:**
- The "So What?" test: For every claim you make, imagine the buyer asking "So what?"
- Building a value proposition that passes the test:
  - Specific (not "we deliver quality")
  - Measurable (include numbers)
  - Relevant to the buyer's industry
  - Differentiated from competitors
  - Credible (backed by evidence)

**Individual Exercise (5 min):** Each participant writes their company's value proposition in ONE sentence using the framework: "We help [specific type of client] achieve [specific outcome] by [specific method], which results in [measurable impact]."

**Share & feedback:** 3–4 participants volunteer to read theirs; trainer provides live coaching.

---

### Session 3.2 — The Perfect Outreach Sequence (10 min)

**Type:** Presentation + Template Walkthrough

**Content:**
- The 3-touch outreach model:
  1. **Research email** (personalized, references their specific challenges, no pitch)
  2. **Value email** (shares a relevant case study or insight)
  3. **Ask email** (specific, time-bound meeting request)
- Templates for each touch
- LinkedIn vs. email vs. warm intro — when to use each
- The importance of timing (budget cycles, Q4 vs. Q1, post-acquisition moments)

---

### Session 3.3 — The Perfect First Meeting (10 min)

**Type:** Presentation

**Content:**
- The 70/30 rule: Listen 70%, talk 30%
- The 5 questions that make buyers trust you immediately:
  1. "What's the biggest challenge your team is facing this quarter?"
  2. "How do you currently solve [specific problem]?"
  3. "What would success look like for this engagement?"
  4. "What went wrong with your previous outsourcing partner?"
  5. "What concerns do you have about working with an external team?"
- How to handle the "send me your rate card" trap
- Following up: the 48-hour rule and what to include in your follow-up

---

### Session 3.4 — Building a Proposal That Wins (15 min)

**Type:** Workshop / Group Exercise

**Exercise: "Proposal Makeover"**

**Setup:** Trainer provides a fictional (but realistic) RFP scenario:
> "LogiCorp is a mid-size logistics company looking for 5 full-stack developers on T&M basis for 12 months to rebuild their customer-facing portal. They use React, Node.js, and AWS. They've had a bad experience with a previous vendor who delivered junior developers positioned as seniors."

**Task (10 min in breakout rooms):**
1. Create a 1-page proposal outline that addresses LogiCorp's explicit AND hidden needs
2. Include: your unique value prop, risk mitigation approach, onboarding plan, quality guarantees, and proposed team composition

**Debrief (5 min):** Groups share key elements; trainer highlights what would make each proposal stand out or fall flat.

## Facilitator Notes

### Energy Management for Online Format:
- Change format every 10–15 minutes (presentation → discussion → exercise → poll)
- Use polls/reactions in Zoom at least once per block
- Call on participants by name to maintain engagement
- Keep camera on and encourage participants to do the same
- Use the Miro board as a shared visual anchor throughout the session

### Breakout Room Tips:
- Always give clear instructions BEFORE opening breakout rooms
- Display a timer on screen
- Visit each breakout room briefly to check progress
- Have a backup plan if groups finish early or need more time

### Difficult Questions to Prepare For:
- "How do I differentiate when everyone has similar skills?" → Focus on process, communication, and domain expertise
- "What if the client's budget is really low?" → Teach them to qualify opportunities and walk away from bad-fit deals
- "How do I sell when I'm a small company with no big case studies?" → Leverage niche expertise and offer pilots
- "Is the T&M model dying?" → No, but it's evolving toward hybrid and outcome-blended models
